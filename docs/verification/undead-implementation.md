# Undead implementation

Retained Castle Fight 9.27/r1 extraction tree: `8ea806dca331ff254995e94e6f0baf225a14bf10`. Builder `X018` ("Undead Builder", `uabi` `Aeth,A06E,AInv,A005,A001,Ahrp,Avul`, `urac` undead) and the generated `catalog-supplement` build-card coordinates define implementation order. Complete each root and its entire upgrade chain before moving right, then the next row. Only completed entities become buildable. The order is Rotting Hay Cart → Tainted Farm → Defiled Granary; Temple of Bone → Temple of Necromancy → High Citadel of Necromancy; Crypt → Graveyard; Haunted Mine; Slaughter House; Dragon Cemetery; Death Pit; Skull Pile → Skull Shrine; Turret of Souls; City of Decay; the shared Treasure Box (already promoted). (`Aeth`, `AInv`, `Ahrp`, `Avul` and the build-menu abilities are the shared builder body already used by every promoted race.)

## Rotting Hay Cart / Zombie

- Identity/source: runtime r207; `h01R` (`hbla`) produces `n00B` (`nzom`) in the top-left slot and upgrades to the Tainted Farm. Protected life, armor, movement and the Chaos melee weapon (ground and structures; the second weapon is disabled and unavailable) come from the catalogs. Corpse data (`can-raise-decays`, with the retained decay timers) is carried by the corpse catalog; Raise Dead is a later Necromancer ability and consumes it unchanged.
- Complete inventory/control flow: the sole ability is `A01L` (`ANpi`, "Permanent Immolation"), registered object data with no script consumer and no hidden proxy. It is the shared native permanent Immolation primitive (`warcraft-permanent-immolation-v1`, also used by the Infernal): radius, damage per interval, interval and target mask come from the retained row. Unlike the Infernal's variant its mask is organic-only, so mechanical bodies are never burned (the recipe projects the `organic` qualifier).
- State: none beyond the shared Immolation pulse phase.
- Undead becomes selectable with this completed root; later roots remain unavailable until their own mechanics are verified.

## Tainted Farm / Suffocated Zombie

## Defiled Granary / Plague Bearer

- Identity/source: runtime r209; `h03M` (`hbla`) upgrades from the Tainted Farm (the end of the line) and produces `u007` (`uabo`). Protected life, armor, movement and the Chaos melee weapon come from the catalogs.
- Complete inventory/control flow: `A05T` (`ANpi`, "Permanent Immolation") and `A05S` (`ACvs`, "Envenomed Weapons"; "Greater Plague" buff) are the same shared native primitives as the Zombie line, with their own retained radius, damage, slow and duration. `A05R` (`Aap1`, "Disease Cloud") is object-native Plague aura data with no script consumer or hidden proxy (registered ability coverage: `native-object-owned`).
- Disease Cloud: the retained row `A05R` gives the carrier's area (350), the infection length (`DataA`, 120 s), the damage per second (`DataB`, 18) and the ward's lifetime (`DataC`, 5 s); its `UnitID1` names the stock plague ward `uplg`. The ward unit and its own aura `Aap2` ("Aura - Plague (Plague Ward)") are not in the map but are registered, hash-pinned base data (`custom_v1/units/abilitydata.slk`; the map never overrides them): area 250, infection length 120 s, 1 damage per second, targets ground enemy organic neutral bodies (no air), buffs `Bapl`/`Bplg`, and a negative ward-duration field — the ward's aura leaves no ward of its own. Trigger/effect eligibility — carrier: hostile non-invulnerable non-spell-immune organic ground or air bodies within 350 of the carrier. Infection — a non-stacking native poison family (buff `Bapl`): damage clock once per second from the first application, lethal, refreshed to the full retained length once a second while the body stays inside an area. Death — an infected body's death leaves the ward at its position for 5 s; the ward belongs to the carrier's team, credits the original carrier, and infects ground organic enemies within 250 with its *own* infection (`Aap2`'s 1 per second for 120 s, buff `Bplg`, kept separate from the carrier's infection so the two never overwrite each other), which spreads no further ward. Resources — free, no cooldown.
- Engine change: `DiseaseCloud` is a new passive primitive over the shared native poison (the poison profile gained an optional plague-ward descriptor carrying the ward's own aura), `PlagueWard` a new authoritative action (canonical tag 44) and the passive hashes tag 46.
- Known discrepancies: the Plague Bearer's authored tooltip says the ward deals 2 spell damage per second and its Rot line 20 per second, which matches neither `A05R`/`A05T` (18 and 2) nor the registered `Aap2` (1); the stock layer of `Aap2` has 2, so the tooltip may predate a data change. Retained data wins. The tooltip also says affected enemies take damage only "until they leave the area", which sits in tension with a fixed 120-second infection; the engine follows the registered lengths. Nothing retained shows whether the aura refresh period is one second, whether ward-infection refreshes while in range, or who is credited for a ward-infected kill (the carrier is).

## Temple of Bone / Necromancer

- Spell trace (`A01Y`): trigger eligibility — a usable (decay start reached) corpse of any team within the retained area (900) of the caster whose death type allows raising (`udea` bit 0; mechanical and cannot-raise bodies are excluded). Approach/cast geometry — none; the order is instant and centered on the caster (the retained `Rng1` 600 is not used by a no-target order; the area is the real reach). Effect origin — each corpse's own position. Effect eligibility — the same corpse test, independent of the trigger: up to the retained count (`DataA`, 1) of the nearest qualifying corpses around the caster, ordered by distance then `SimId`, are consumed and a skeleton is created at each for the caster's owner and team: the Necromancer draws uniformly from the retained table's first three entries (Skeleton Warrior, Archer, Mage), the Mighty Necromancer from its first seven, the Lich King from a one-in-eight General branch otherwise uniformly over entries four to seven. The corpse catalog now retains the map's raise flag for production bodies and summon bodies (supplement schema field `can_raise`).
- Known discrepancies: the importer's second unit type (`DataD`, a placeholder) is ignored as it names no body; whether the original AI cast Raise Dead from a corpse beyond the 600 cast range cannot be checked without a Warcraft runtime oracle.

## High Citadel of Necromancy / Lich King

- Identity/source: runtime r212; `h055` (`hbla`) upgrades from the Temple of Necromancy (the end of the line) and produces `u00D` (`unec`). Protected life, armor, movement, the mana pool (400, starting 250) and the Magic missile weapon (ground, structures, air; range 500) come from the catalogs. The carrier `u00E` ("Lich Ghoul") is replaced by a skeleton; replacements additionally receive the permanent `A088` (+12 attack) and `A089` (+3 armor) grants.
- Complete inventory/control flow: `A0CV`, `A08X` and `A07H` are hidden Channel markers (the last is `TypeLegendary`); `A06V` is the shared legendary Sphere marker and `A070` the legendary Moon Glaive marker, which the devour branch reads as an immunity; `A0AH` is the shared native spell-resistance row (one recipe serves every legendary bearer); `A03Q` is the shared source-damage dispatcher marker, which gates `handleSourceDamageEffects` for this body; `A085` (`Arai`) is the Necromancer's Raise Dead with the retained count of two, its own raised body, protected mana cost (100) and cooldown (25 s); `A086` (`AUdd`, "Death And Decay") is registered object data that no order of the unit ever issues — the script grants it to a dummy.
- Scripted spell trace (`devourAttackProc`, dispatched by the damage listener for source type `u00D` while the bearer carries `A03Q`): trigger eligibility — each landed weapon hit (including a hit that kills its target) rolls one integer in 0..99 against two thresholds; the roll is partitioned: below 15 and an eligible target devours, otherwise below 25 (including an ineligible target that rolled below 15) releases Death and Decay. Both branches require the victim to be a combat body: the script's damage listener is registered per unit only for combat bodies that enter the map, never for structures, Ghouls or builders. Devour eligibility — such a living, non-mechanical unit not carrying the legendary marker `A070`. Devour effect — the bearer regains half of the target's current life (before the damage) up to its maximum, then the target takes the retained 10000 chaos/death attack damage, which ignores armor (invulnerable bodies take none). The killing blow is itself an attack-flagged damage event that re-enters the unguarded listener while the target still has its life, so the roll repeats: each further roll below the devour threshold heals once more (all from the same untouched life) and nests again, and the chain ends at the first roll that does not devour, which may itself release Death and Decay. The Death and Decay spell is cast by a dummy unit (type `e008`, owned by the Lich King's player, timed life 6 s) at the damaged target's position at hit time; effect eligibility is independent: hostile air or ground units (the map removed stock's structure token, so the retained building reduction never applies and structures are not damaged) inside the retained area (350), each losing the retained 8% of its maximum life once per second for the retained five seconds. Invulnerable bodies are never affected, but spell-immune ones are (the spell is an ultimate; see the engine verification below); each affected body pulses on its own phase (see the engine verification below), and the damage is credited to the bearer in this port (the engine credits the caster handle of the area, the dummy `e008`, which belongs to the same player). Resources — none. Persistence — the area is an authoritative action that outlives the target and the bearer.
- Engine change: `DeathMastery` is a new passive primitive resolved at the attack roll into a triggered attack effect (canonical tag 8, passive tag 47); `DeathAndDecay` a new authoritative action (canonical tag 45). The devour kills and heals through the ordinary health commit with source credit. Snapshot schema 111, checksum schema 108.
- Engine verification (Warcraft III `Game.dll`, August 2017 build, image base `0x6f000000`, analysed in Ghidra through its MSVC RTTI): the spell is `CAbilityDeathAndDecay` (vtable `0x6fb367f8`), its area `CEffectDeathAndDecay` (`0x6fb320fc`, derived from the Tranquility range-listener effect) and the per-body buff `CBuffDeathAndDecayAoe` (`0x6fb36474`, derived from `CBuffAoe`). (1) Attaching the buff (`0x6f61bbc0`) stores the caster handle and the per-pulse life fraction and arms the buff's timer with a random delay drawn uniformly from the first second (PRNG stream 0x16); the pulse handler (`0x6f6239c0`) re-arms it with the shared one-second constant (`0x6fd04354`, also the damage-over-time period getter `0x6f518db0`), so each body inside the area pulses once per second on its own phase and a body that stays the whole five seconds loses its share five times. (2) The pulse deals the target's maximum life times the stored fraction through `CUnit::Damage` (`0x6f6cf530`) with damage-kind bit `0x4000000`, attack type 0. The kind bits are `1 << jass damage type` (`0x10` Normal for attacks, `0x4000` Magic for Mana Burn and Lightning Purge, `0x2000` Divine for Holy Bolt, `0x1000000` Spirit Link), so `0x4000000` is Universal, the same family as the Whirlwind and Starfall ports. (3) With the stock `MagicImmunesResistDamage=1` the damage routine's two immunity masks (`0x033ee700`, the magic types, and `0x00c11830`, the physical types) both exclude Universal, so the hit is never blocked there; immunity acts instead when the area picks its bodies: the range-listener filter (`0x6f5b6e50`, shared by the Tranquility, Starfall and Death and Decay effects) calls the target-validity routine (`0x6f6a6020`), which rejects a body with a magic-immunity count (error `0x36`) for an ability whose harmful flag is set (the ability's virtual `0x1d8` returns 1 here), with two escapes that apply to this spell: the first is the game constant `MagicImmunesResistUltimates` (the string at `0x6fb68de8`, group `Misc`, read by `0x6f0712d0`) and the second the ability virtual `0x20c` (`0x6f451c60`, shared by every ability class), which is true when the ability row's second field after `levels` (row offset `0x40`, the `reqLevel` column of the ability-data field table at `0x6fcb41f0`, default 1) is above 1. With the constant at 0 an ultimate passes immunity; with 1 it is rejected. The constant is 0 in `base` and `custom_v1` (the Default TFT set this map resolves to) and the map's `war3mapMisc.txt` does not override it (`custom_v0` and `melee_v0` set it to 1). Death and Decay (`A086`, `reqLevel` 6) and Starfall (`A07T`/`A08T`, `reqLevel` 6) therefore both reach spell-immune bodies, matching the community reports. Ethereal bodies pass a separate check (`0x6f6a60f0`; `0x1dc`/`0x1f4` return 0). Neither the buff attach (`0x6f61bbc0`) nor the pulse handler (`0x6f6239c0`) tests immunity again. Port: the per-body phase is a deterministic draw in 1..=29 ticks after the release (the engine's continuous delay in [0, 1) s cannot fire on the release tick itself), repeated every 30 ticks while the area stands; eligibility is checked at each body's own pulse rather than on range-listener events, so a body that becomes immune or leaves between pulses is spared at its next pulse and one that enters keeps the area's phase for it. Snapshot schema 119, checksum schema 116.
- Armor handling (Ghidra, `Game.dll`, `0x6f6cf530`, the virtual damage routine; damage record fields: `[3]` flags, `[4]` amount, `[5]` kind bit, `[8]` attack type): the numeric armor-point reduction (the `0x6fd38698` table lookup) runs only when the kind is exactly Normal (`0x10`), so Universal (`0x4000000`) takes no armor-point reduction. The attack-type by armor-type multiplier (`DamageBonus*` tables at `0x6fd38708`, indexed `attackType*8 + armorType`) runs for every kind. Engine attack-type indices are 0 Spells, 1 Normal, 2 Pierce, 3 Siege, 4 Magic, 5 Chaos, 6 Hero, so the pulse's attack type 0 selects the `DamageBonusSpells` row, which is what `DamageRules::apply_spell` already applies. The port matches on both points. Defend and Magic Defense listeners are a separate question; see `engine-evidence-gaps.md`.
- Known discrepancies: the devour heal rounds down; the recursive chain is capped at 16 nested casts (probability 0.15^16 beyond it).

## City of Decay

- Identity/source: runtime r221; `h01Q` (`hbla`) is a utility building in the second slot of the third row; no weapon, production or upgrade. Protected life, armor, collision and construction/repair/economy come from the catalogs.
- Complete inventory/control flow: `A06V` (`Asph`, "Legendary Unit") is the shared legendary marker and `A01T` (`ANpi`, "UND: City Of Decay") the shared native permanent Immolation, registered object data with no script consumer or hidden proxy. `A01T` has the map-wide retained area (20000), the retained 10 damage and the stock one-second interval, on enemy air or ground organic bodies (neutral included); the Immolation buff family is `B003`.
- Effect trace: origin — the building's center, from the tick it finishes construction (an unfinished building does nothing). Eligibility — alive hostile organic non-invulnerable, non-spell-immune, non-Locust bodies anywhere (the radius covers the map); damage is spell damage credited to the building, applied once per interval for as long as the building stands.
- Engine change: the shared permanent Immolation now also runs from completed buildings (it previously ran from units only).
- Stacking: the building's tooltip states that several copies give no additional benefit, and the design treats that as authoritative. No script, build limit or buff rule in the retained data enforces it (the only built-in limiter is the default legendary food allowance of one building per player), so the engine enforces it: only the oldest standing City of Decay of a team (lowest id) contributes for the ability, and when it falls the next oldest takes over on its own clock. The other team's building is an independent source against the opposite side. Confirmed in-game by the project owner (several City of Decay buildings do not stack).
